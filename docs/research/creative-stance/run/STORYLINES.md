# Storylines - "Why agentic engineering"

The one idea: **agents now do the cooking; the engineer's job is building the check that every plate passes before it leaves - because you can't feel whether it worked.**

The misconception to break: *"If the AI writes the code, the engineering part matters less - just accept what it gives you."*

Ideas sketched before choosing (device × form):
1. Kitchen service, overhead cooking-show cam (object is the explanation × one world, camera travels) - **picked**
2. Game show "Faster or Slower?", viewer is the contestant (question with the input × cut on the beat)
3. Courtroom: The People v. Vibe Coding, viewer is the jury (life before/after × split screen to one)
4. Nature documentary of a codebase, agents as foraging animals (character does the work × endless zoom)
5. Air-traffic control tower (one object across apps × flight through one world) - dropped: it reads as "monitoring dashboards", the HUD look the BANNED list forbids
6. Heist crew, the engineer is the planner (character × match cuts) - dropped: a heist celebrates getting away with it, the opposite of verification

## A. THE PASS (recommended - built)
Device: the object is the explanation. Form: one overhead camera over one restaurant kitchen during Friday service, pushing into tickets, plates and the probe thermometer, never leaving the kitchen.
The viewer is on their first night at the pass. Tickets are software tasks, the line cooks are AI agents, and the rookie beside you thinks the chef is now pointless. The pass - where a plate is checked before it goes out - is literally the agent loop: ticket → cook with tools → plate → probe → send back or serve.
- First 2 s: a thermal printer chatters and a ticket curls onto the rail reading `FIX: CHECKOUT BUG`.
- Beats: service starts → rookie "just send it all" → plates fly out unchecked → CALL IT: faster or slower? → 19% slower, felt 20% faster, rookie's ticket crumples → tickets keep getting longer (×2 / 7 months) → the empty pass → build the loop, plate fails the probe, goes back, passes, bell → where the probe lies → the name → "Building the pass is the job".
- Look: brushed stainless, white subway tile, white thermal tickets, masking-tape labels in marker. Red = sent back, green = passed, amber = time.
- Sound: kitchen room tone (no music), printer = new ticket, plate on steel = work handed in, bell = passed the check, plate scrape = sent back, kitchen timer = the viewer's guess. Bed drops out under the loop; returns with the sizzle when service runs right.
Why: the metaphor *is* the mechanism (a kitchen pass is a real verification loop), the misconception has a body (the rookie), and the viewer is asked twice before being told.

## B. FASTER OR SLOWER? (game show)
Device: a question with the input. Form: studio game show, hard cuts on the stings.
The viewer is the contestant; a rival contestant, "Vibe", slams the FASTER buzzer on every question. Each round is a real study result; the final round is "which developer shipped working code?"
- First 2 s: lights hit a podium with the viewer's name blank, the host: "Round one."
- Beats: rules → round 1 METR guess → round 2 time horizons → bonus round: the loop → Vibe loses → title.
- Look: stage plywood, bulb-ringed boards, scoreboard flip digits. Sound: buzzers, crowd, a stinger per round.
Not built: the prediction device is strong, but the mechanism (the loop) has no home in a game show; it would fall back to a slide behind the host.

## C. NATURE DOCUMENTARY: THE AGENT IN THE WILD (the bold one)
Device: a character does the work. Form: endless zoom into a codebase landscape filmed like wildlife.
A hushed narrator follows a young agent through a repository "habitat": it forages for context, gets lost without a test to drink from, and thrives only where a keeper (the engineer) has built a watering hole of tests.
- First 2 s: long-lens shot of a file tree as grassland at dawn, a small creature lifting its head.
- Beats: habitat → the hunt → the mirage (feels fast, is slow) → the keeper builds the watering hole → herd thrives → title.
- Look: hand-painted savanna in code-editor colours. Sound: field ambience, narrator whisper.
Not built: funniest of the three, but "an agent is an animal" breaks too early (agents don't learn between sessions) and the engineer becomes a zookeeper, which undersells the craft.
