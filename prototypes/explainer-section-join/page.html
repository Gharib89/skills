Section join on dora-core

Does a long video render per section and join cleanly? Twelve dora-core scenes (hook to infra, 5 min 14 s), cut into three sections opened by hook, structure and flow, each rendered on its own and joined with ffmpeg -f concat -c copy, against the same twelve scenes rendered whole.

Yes, once a section starts on a frame boundary.

The concat is frame-exact; the risk sits in the timeline. The composer times scenes to the millisecond, so a section opener falls between frames (structure starts at frame 2965.05). Each section renders ceil(duration × 30) frames, and the joined video gains up to one frame per join. Round every scene's duration up to whole frames and the joined video equals the whole render frame for frame: chapters land on the opener, captions on the right frame, and the audio stays sample-exact when it is mixed once over the joined video.

One cell is one frame (33.3 ms). Naive: structure is timed at 98.835 s, between frames 2965 and 2966, so section 1 renders 2966 frames and section 2's first frame (structure at its own time 0) goes out at frame 2966 against an audio clock that reached structure 0.95 frame earlier. Snapped: every scene's duration rounded up to whole frames moves structure to 98.900 s, exactly frame 2967, and the narration is padded with the same silence.

Frames at the joins, snapped
Two smaller finds

A time written a hair long gains a frame. HyperFrames renders ceil(duration × fps) frames, so a frame-exact duration printed as 104.966667 (3149.00001 frames) renders 3150. The prototype's first build lost one frame that way; it now truncates. The 53 lossless differences are the same effect on caption words: in section 3, whose offset 203.8666... s never terminates, a word time shifted to the whole timeline and written to six places rounds past its frame, so the highlight shows one frame later in the whole render than in the section. Times on the frame grid want integer frames, not decimals.

HyperFrames' own --resume does not do this job. Its segment reuse is keyed by a hash of the whole composition (read from source, computeSegmentPlanHash), so it resumes a crashed render but reuses nothing after an edit.

Your call

What the spec carries from this

  1. Time every scene on the frame grid. RECOMMENDED
    Each scene's duration rounds up to whole frames, its WAV padded with that silence, so every scene start (and with it every section opener, still and gate sample) is a whole frame. Cost: under 33 ms per scene; these 12 scenes grew 0.18 s. The alternative, snapping only section openers, keeps two timing rules in the composer for no saving.
  2. Sections render video only and join with a stream copy; the audio is mixed and AAC-encoded once on the joined file, with the chapters.
    This is the shape the long-video ticket chose; the probe confirms it and rules out encoding audio per section (44 ms, then 80 ms off).
  3. The determinism gate compares a section with itself.
    It holds per section. The joined file is not bit-identical to a whole render under lossy encoding, and nothing should test that.
  4. Times are integer frames inside the composer, written out truncated, never rounded up.
